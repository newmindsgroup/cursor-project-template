# Persuasion Principles Checklist

Map the six principles of ethical persuasion to website sections. This checklist ensures every page leverages proven psychological triggers while maintaining integrity and authenticity.

## Purpose

Audit and enhance website persuasion elements to:
- Ensure trust-building elements are present throughout
- Identify missing persuasion triggers by section
- Guide content and design decisions with psychology
- Create ethical urgency without manipulation

## When to Use

- During content strategy and copywriting
- When reviewing landing page effectiveness
- Before launching new pages or campaigns
- As part of conversion rate optimization (CRO)
- During QA review of messaging

## The Six Principles of Ethical Persuasion

These principles describe how people naturally make decisions. Use them to help customers make decisions that are good for them—never to manipulate.

---

## Principle 1: Reciprocity

**Definition:** People feel obligated to give back when they receive something first.

### Website Application

```
AUDIT CHECKLIST

Lead Generation:
- [ ] Free lead magnet available (guide, checklist, template)
- [ ] Lead magnet provides genuine value
- [ ] No strings attached feeling
- [ ] Easy to access (minimal form fields)

Content Value:
- [ ] Blog/resource section with free insights
- [ ] Tools or calculators available
- [ ] Video content or tutorials
- [ ] Free consultation or assessment offered

Trust Building:
- [ ] Valuable information given before asking for sale
- [ ] Free samples or trials where applicable
- [ ] Surprise bonuses mentioned
- [ ] Thank you/follow-up value delivered
```

### Section Mapping

| Section | Reciprocity Element |
|---------|---------------------|
| Hero | Free resource CTA (transitional) |
| Resources | Educational content library |
| Footer | Newsletter with value promise |
| Pop-ups | Exit intent with free offer |
| Thank You | Bonus resource delivery |

### Implementation Examples

```
Strong: "Download our free 10-page guide to [solving problem]"
Weak: "Sign up for our newsletter"

Strong: "Get a free 30-minute strategy session"
Weak: "Contact us for a quote"

Strong: "Here's exactly how we [achieved result] - full breakdown"
Weak: "We're experts in [service]"
```

## Principle 2: Commitment and Consistency

**Definition:** People align future actions with past commitments and self-image.

### Website Application

```
AUDIT CHECKLIST

Micro-Commitments:
- [ ] Small first steps available (not just "buy now")
- [ ] Progressive disclosure in forms
- [ ] Quiz or assessment that engages
- [ ] Newsletter signup before sales pitch

Identity Alignment:
- [ ] Content speaks to who they want to be
- [ ] "People like you" language used
- [ ] Values alignment messaging
- [ ] Community/belonging elements

Consistency Triggers:
- [ ] Previous commitment acknowledgment
- [ ] Public commitment options (reviews, shares)
- [ ] Progress indicators in multi-step processes
- [ ] Confirmation of choices made
```

### Section Mapping

| Section | Commitment Element |
|---------|-------------------|
| Hero | Low-commitment CTA option |
| How It Works | Step 1 is small and easy |
| Forms | Progressive fields, save progress |
| Pricing | Start with entry option |
| Checkout | Confirmation of choices |

### Implementation Examples

```
Strong: "Start with a free assessment" → "Based on your assessment..." → "Here's your custom plan"
Weak: "Buy now"

Strong: "You've already taken the first step by [action]. Here's what's next..."
Weak: "Ready to get started?"

Strong: "As someone who values [value], you'll appreciate..."
Weak: "Our product has these features..."
```

## Principle 3: Social Proof

**Definition:** People follow the actions of others, especially similar others.

### Website Application

```
AUDIT CHECKLIST

Testimonials:
- [ ] Customer testimonials present (with photos/names)
- [ ] Testimonials are specific (results, not just praise)
- [ ] Testimonials from relatable customers
- [ ] Video testimonials where possible
- [ ] Testimonials address common objections

Numbers and Stats:
- [ ] Customer/client count displayed
- [ ] Results statistics shown
- [ ] Industry recognition mentioned
- [ ] Social media follower counts (if impressive)

Third-Party Validation:
- [ ] Client logos displayed
- [ ] Media mentions/features shown
- [ ] Awards and certifications
- [ ] Industry associations
- [ ] Reviews from external platforms

Real-Time Proof:
- [ ] Recent activity notifications
- [ ] Live customer count
- [ ] Recent reviews displayed
- [ ] Social media feed integration
```

### Section Mapping

| Section | Social Proof Element |
|---------|---------------------|
| Hero | Key stat or client logos |
| Features | "Used by X companies" |
| Testimonials | Full testimonial section |
| Pricing | "Most popular" badge |
| Footer | Trust badges, review scores |

### Implementation Examples

```
Strong: "Sarah increased her revenue by 47% in 90 days" [photo, company]
Weak: "Great service!" - John

Strong: "Join 2,847 business owners who've transformed their marketing"
Weak: "Many customers trust us"

Strong: "Featured in Forbes, Inc, and Entrepreneur"
Weak: "We've been in business for 10 years"
```

## Principle 4: Authority

### Website Application

```
AUDIT CHECKLIST

- [ ] Years of experience mentioned
- [ ] Specialized knowledge highlighted
- [ ] Industry expertise evident
- [ ] Educational content demonstrates depth

Results and Track Record:
- [ ] Case studies with specific outcomes
- [ ] Portfolio of past work
- [ ] Success metrics shared
- [ ] Before/after examples
- [ ] Client results highlighted

Third-Party Authority:
- [ ] Media features and quotes
- [ ] Speaking engagements mentioned
- [ ] Books or publications authored
- [ ] Industry awards received
- [ ] Professional memberships

Visual Authority:
- [ ] Professional photography
- [ ] High-quality design
- [ ] Consistent branding
```

### Section Mapping

|---------|------------------|

## Principle 5: Liking

**Definition:** People say yes to those they know, like, and trust.

### Website Application

```
AUDIT CHECKLIST

Relatability:
- [ ] Team photos with personalities shown
- [ ] Origin story/founder journey shared
- [ ] Behind-the-scenes content
- [ ] Authentic, conversational voice
- [ ] Shared values highlighted

Similarity:
- [ ] Language matches target audience
- [ ] Examples relevant to their world
- [ ] Cultural alignment evident
- [ ] "People like us" messaging
- [ ] Shared struggles acknowledged

Warmth:
- [ ] Friendly, approachable tone
- [ ] Humor where appropriate
- [ ] Personal touches throughout
- [ ] Genuine care expressed
- [ ] Empathy statements present

Visual Appeal:
- [ ] Attractive, modern design
- [ ] Pleasant color palette
- [ ] Professional but approachable imagery
- [ ] Consistent visual identity
```

### Section Mapping

| Section | Liking Element |
|---------|---------------|
| Hero | Warm, welcoming message |
| About | Team personalities, story |
| Content | Conversational voice |
| Testimonials | Relatable customer stories |
| Contact | Personal, inviting approach |

### Implementation Examples

```
Strong: "We started this company after struggling with the same problem you're facing..."
Weak: "Our company was founded in 2015"

Strong: "Hey, we get it. Marketing feels overwhelming when you're already wearing 47 hats."
Weak: "Marketing can be challenging for business owners."

Strong: [Photo of real team in natural setting with genuine smiles]
Weak: [Stock photo of generic business people]
```

## Principle 6: Scarcity

**Definition:** People value what's rare, limited, or disappearing.

### Website Application

```
AUDIT CHECKLIST

Time Scarcity:
- [ ] Limited-time offers (genuine)
- [ ] Countdown timers (for real deadlines)
- [ ] Early-bird pricing
- [ ] Seasonal availability
- [ ] Application deadlines

Quantity Scarcity:
- [ ] Limited spots available
- [ ] Exclusive membership limits
- [ ] Limited edition offerings
- [ ] Waitlist for popular items
- [ ] Inventory indicators

Exclusive Access:
- [ ] VIP or premium tiers
- [ ] Members-only content
- [ ] First access for subscribers
- [ ] Invite-only options
- [ ] Beta or founding member status

Information Scarcity:
- [ ] Exclusive insights offered
- [ ] Proprietary methods mentioned
- [ ] Unique data or research
- [ ] Insider knowledge promised
```

### Section Mapping

| Section | Scarcity Element |
|---------|-----------------|
| Hero | Limited offer banner |
| Pricing | "Only X spots left" |
| CTA | Deadline or urgency |
| Forms | "Join X others waiting" |
| Checkout | Stock/availability indicator |

### Implementation Examples

```
Strong: "We only accept 5 new clients per month to ensure quality" (if true)
Weak: "Limited time offer!" (when it's always available)

Strong: "Founding member pricing ends January 31st" (real deadline)
Weak: "Sale ends soon!"

Strong: "237 people have downloaded this guide today"
Weak: "Popular download"
```

### Ethical Scarcity Warning

**Never use fake scarcity.** False deadlines, fake "only 3 left" counters, and manufactured urgency destroy trust. Only use scarcity when:
- The limitation is real
- The deadline is genuine
- The scarcity is honest
